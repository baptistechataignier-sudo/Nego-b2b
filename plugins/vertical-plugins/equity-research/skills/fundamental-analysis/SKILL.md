# Fundamental Analysis

## Overview

Fundamental analysis is the process of evaluating a company's intrinsic value by examining the underlying business — its competitive position, financial performance, management quality, and growth prospects — independent of short-term market price movements. The goal is to determine whether a stock is undervalued, fairly valued, or overvalued relative to its fundamental worth.

## Step 1: Business Model Analysis

Before looking at any numbers, understand how the company makes money.

**Questions to answer:**
1. What does the company sell, and to whom?
2. How does the company acquire customers? (Sales-led, product-led, channel, direct-to-consumer)
3. How does the company retain customers? (Switching costs, contracts, network effects, habit)
4. What is the unit of revenue? (Per seat, per transaction, per pound, per hour)
5. Is revenue recurring or transactional? (Subscription ARR vs. project revenue vs. product sales)
6. What are the primary cost drivers? (People, raw materials, logistics, cloud infrastructure)
7. What does the marginal unit economics look like? (Gross margin tells you this)

**Revenue quality spectrum (highest to lowest):**
1. Multi-year contracted ARR (SaaS with annual contracts)
2. Monthly recurring revenue (month-to-month SaaS, utilities)
3. Repeat transactional revenue (high-frequency with strong retention)
4. Project/contract revenue (lumpy; depends on winning new contracts)
5. One-time product sales (no guarantee of repeat purchase)

## Step 2: Competitive Moat Assessment

A company with a durable competitive advantage (moat) can earn returns on capital above its cost of capital for longer periods. Identify which moat sources are present:

### Moat Sources

**Network Effects** — The product becomes more valuable as more users join. Test: does CAC fall as the network grows? Does churn fall as usage increases? Examples: marketplaces, payment networks, communication platforms.

**Switching Costs** — Customers incur significant cost (financial, operational, or psychological) to switch to a competitor. Test: what is the all-in cost for a customer to replace this product? Examples: ERP software, core banking systems, industrial sensors with proprietary protocols.

**Cost Advantages** — The company can produce its product at a meaningfully lower cost than competitors. Sources: scale economies, process advantages, proprietary technology, cheaper access to inputs. Test: is COGS as a % of revenue declining as the company scales? Is it structurally lower than competitors?

**Intangible Assets** — Brands, patents, regulatory licenses, or proprietary data that competitors cannot easily replicate. Test: can the company price above competitors without losing market share?

**Efficient Scale** — The company operates in a market where adding a competitor would be uneconomical for all participants (regional monopolies, natural monopolies, niche markets where the market supports only a few players).

Rate each moat source as: None / Narrow / Wide

| Moat Source | Present? | Strength | Evidence |
|-------------|----------|----------|----------|

A company with 2+ Wide moat sources is a rare, high-quality business. Most companies have at best narrow moats in 1-2 areas.

## Step 3: Financial Quality Assessment

### Earnings Quality

High-quality earnings are sustainable, recurring, and backed by cash. Low-quality earnings can disappear.

**Accruals analysis:**
- Cash earnings quality ratio = Operating Cash Flow / Net Income
- Ratio > 1.0: Cash conversion is strong; earnings are backed by cash
- Ratio < 0.7: Earnings are running ahead of cash; investigate working capital build, revenue recognition, or capitalized expenses
- Ratio consistently < 1.0 over 3+ years: Red flag for earnings manipulation or business model deterioration

**Revenue recognition:**
- Are deferred revenue balances growing? (Good sign — customers paying in advance)
- Are accounts receivable growing faster than revenue? (Bad sign — may indicate lenient credit terms or pull-forward of revenue)
- DSO trend: rising DSO means customers are paying more slowly; declining DSO is a positive quality signal

**Margin sustainability:**
- What is driving gross margin? Is it structural (product mix, pricing power) or transient (one-time customer mix, favorable commodity cycle)?
- Are operating expenses scaling sublinearly with revenue? (Positive operating leverage)
- Are capex requirements increasing? (Rising capex intensity reduces free cash flow and ROIC)

### Return on Invested Capital (ROIC)

ROIC = NOPAT / Invested Capital
- NOPAT = EBIT × (1 − effective tax rate)
- Invested Capital = Total equity + Total debt − Cash and equivalents

**Why ROIC matters:** A company that earns ROIC > WACC creates value. A company with ROIC < WACC destroys value regardless of growth.

**ROIC interpretation:**
- ROIC > 20%: Exceptional — typically indicates significant competitive advantage
- ROIC 12-20%: Good business with some competitive positioning
- ROIC 8-12%: Average; value creation depends on cost of capital
- ROIC < WACC: Value-destroying; growth makes the situation worse

## Step 4: Management Quality Assessment

**Capital allocation track record:**
- What has management done with free cash flow? (Organic reinvestment, acquisitions, buybacks, dividends)
- Have acquisitions created or destroyed value? (Check post-acquisition ROIC, integration results)
- Is management buying back stock opportunistically (when undervalued) or indiscriminately?

**Execution vs. guidance:**
- Does management consistently hit or beat its own guidance?
- How has management handled periods of adversity? (Cost discipline, communication quality)
- Are incentives aligned? (Management compensation structure — are they paid on ROIC, FCF, or just revenue growth?)

**Communication quality:**
- Earnings calls: Does management explain the business clearly, or use jargon to obscure underperformance?
- Letter to shareholders: Does management acknowledge mistakes and discuss capital allocation explicitly?

## Step 6: Investment Thesis Summary

Synthesize into a 1-page investment thesis:

1. **The Elevator Pitch** — Why does this stock go up? One or two sentences.
2. **Business Quality** — Moat assessment, revenue quality, ROIC history
3. **Growth Drivers** — What will drive revenue and earnings growth over the next 3-5 years?
4. **Key Risks** — The 2-3 risks that could invalidate the thesis
5. **Variant View** — What does the market misunderstand or underappreciate? (If the stock is efficiently priced, there's no edge)
6. **Valuation** — See the `valuation` skill for price target methodology
7. **Catalyst** — What events in the next 6-12 months could re-rate the stock?
